I'm Jack (Yuanfan) Ying ([d͡ʒæk (jwɛn˨˩fɐn˥˥) jɪŋ˥˥]), a postdoctoral fellow at the Brain and Mind Institute and Department of Linguistics and Modern Languages at the Chinese University of Hong Kong. I received my PhD in Linguistics from the University of Maryland, College Park, advised by Jeffrey Lidz and Alexander Williams, and also worked with Valentine Hacquard and Kate Mooney.

I'm a native speaker of Mandarin Chinese and Tai-chow Wu (partially intelligible with Shanghainese). Outside of research, I love music, movies, hiking, and kayaking.

Research Interest

My work sits at the intersection of language development and language processing. I use corpus, experimental, and computational methods to understand how language learning happens and evolves, with interests spanning syntax, semantics, pragmatics, phonetics, and phonology/tonology. I am particularly interested in connecting experimental evidence and linguistic theories with computational approaches—including with large language models—to understand what makes human learning different from machine learning.
